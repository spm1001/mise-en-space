## For Claudes to come

**A heuristic's false-positive rate is a measurement, not a design property — and in this repo it costs one probe script.** The pattern that worked three times today: `get_sync_client()` + the Gmail API against the authed mailbox gives you a live corpus in ~20 lines (`category:promotions OR category:updates` for notification-shaped mail); run the candidate predicate over real payloads BEFORE shipping, and keep the true positive (thread 19fb9faca1565748 for tables) in the loop as the must-still-fire control. The first `has_data_table` shipped on design intent and measured 31/40 wrong; the recalibration measured 0/35 before it shipped. Corollary for prose: writing "accepted false positive" without a number is the tell — the phrase should trigger the probe.

**Structure-discard is one disease with many hosts.** Outlook's auto-generated text/plain flattens tables before mise ever sees the message (fingerprints: `*` bullets with two-space indent, `<angle-bracket>` URLs, `[cid:...]` placeholders — seeing those means you're reading the plain part). Our own HTML cleaner did it by deleting empty `<td>`s (cost asymmetry ruled: a kept spacer is cosmetic, a deleted data cell is silent corruption). markitdown does it to PDFs two ways, split-flattening (loud) and delimiter-stripping (quiet — rows become plausible sentences of numbers). The quiet variants are the dangerous ones, and the discriminating fact for prose-vs-table lines: prose writes figures with unit suffixes ("£3,214m"), tables write them bare ("3,214").

**Gmail MCP architecture fact worth keeping:** stdio plugin servers are one-per-Claude-session, version fixed at spawn from the then-installed cache dir, dying with their session. Four Claudes = four servers; two can share a version and they're still separate processes. `ps -eo args | grep cache/batterie/mise` settles who runs what — but never through `sort -u`, which is how this session briefly believed one-server-per-version.
