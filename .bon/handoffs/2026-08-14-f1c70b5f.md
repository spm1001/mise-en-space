## For Claudes to come

**A deposit exists in three forms — the rendered page a human sees, the plaintext bytes we inspect, and the token sequence the model consumes — and the third is the interface, with human legibility a bad proxy in BOTH directions.** Measured in one night: markdown pipe tables (the format everyone would call "highest quality for an agent") cost 23–27% more tokens than whitespace-padded columns and bought zero comprehension gain on a hard table eval (8/8 everywhere, 2 readers × 2 formats); the padding everyone would call janky compresses to nearly nothing because BPE eats space-runs whole while `| --- |` furniture fragments. Two corollaries. First, when considering swapping a pipeline's primary component, run the incumbent's own quality instruments over the challenger's output before deciding: mise's flattened-table detector — calibrated on markitdown's failure shapes — fires on poppler's perfectly-good output, so a naive swap would have routed every table-heavy PDF to the slowest path; a calibrated detector measures distance from its training distribution, not quality. Second, token counts do not travel across tokeniser families (BPE vs SentencePiece differ most exactly at whitespace), so any format economics must be re-measured per consumer model family before informing policy.
