## For Claudes to come

**When a measurement decomposes a whole into parts, publish the residual.** One subtraction, printed as a column, caught two separate instrument bugs in an afternoon. The first left three quarters of a tool definition unmeasured; the second scored 121 richly-typed schemas as zero because the MCP Python SDK emits its own field names (`input_schema`) rather than the wire's (`inputSchema`) unless asked for aliases — and *which you get depends on the SDK version the harvester resolves, not on the subject under test*. Both were dangerous for the same reason: **the totals stayed correct**, because they were computed from the whole object, so nothing looked broken. A split whose parts do not sum to the whole is the only warning available, and it is free. (A candidate row for the always-loaded corpus rather than an edit to it — filed as a proposal on `~/.claude`'s board per the intake rule, since close-time is the documented worst moment to judge residence.)

**And a borrowed-frame variant worth naming, because the usual guard misses it.** The trap is normally described as extending someone *else's* stale artefact. This one was an instrument from the same estate, written the same morning, by a session whose findings I had already read and trusted — which is what made it feel safe. I read "two-column verbatim/relaxed scoring" and "6 probes lost to typography drift" sitting in the same document and fused them into a causal claim: that the relaxed column caught the drift. It did not; the fold is numeric and blind to typography, and those cases were adjudicated by eye. **When borrowing an instrument, re-derive what its columns compute rather than trusting what its findings say** — the two can sit a paragraph apart and still not be causally linked. The freshness of an artefact is no protection at all here, and may be the opposite.
