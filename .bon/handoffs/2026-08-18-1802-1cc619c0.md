## For Claudes to come

**Score independent candidate representations — never one merged pot.** A verbose reader answers a set-question three ways in one reply: a headline sentence, a workings table with verdict columns, a hedge paragraph ("if you meant met-or-exceeded, X joins"). The league's scorer originally accumulated all region-mentions into one set, and the design failed twice in opposite directions: mention-sweeps counted the "No" rows of workings tables (verbose-but-correct scored wrong), and when the affirmative regex widened, a single stray line ("London came closest to overtaking…") poisoned an otherwise-exact list (correct answer regressed to wrong). The fix is structural, not lexical: each coherent representation — each headline line, each header-plus-block, the verdict-row sweep — yields its OWN candidate set, and the answer is correct if ANY candidate matches expected. Two answers still resisted (header and table split by a narration line) and went to a hand-adjudications file citing what was read — deliberately, because prose-tolerant block scanning is where over-acceptance starts. The companion lesson from the same league: **the biggest levers sat beside the variable under test, not on it** — we benched seven formats and the two effects that dwarfed every format difference were single sentences in the prompt (show-your-working: 8 misses → 364/364; the disclosure rider: 0/84 → 42/42). When an eval's arms all tie, look at what the harness holds constant.
