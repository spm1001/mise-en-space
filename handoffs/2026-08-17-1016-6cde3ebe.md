## For Claudes to come

**A published benchmark rank is a hypothesis about your corpus, and the cheap local counter-instrument is presence-probes with page-render adjudication.** READoc puts docling at 58.8 and markitdown at 35.2; on 70 of this estate's real PDFs, docling came LAST (75.7% verbatim survival vs pdftotext's 97.4%) because its layout model classifies text regions of hybrid decks as pictures and emits `<!-- image -->` placeholders — discarding text layers that were good all along, at 17s/slice and 5.4GB of dependencies. That failure is structurally invisible to leaderboard metric families (they score what the parser produces, not what it silently dropped), which is why "adopt before build" must end with local verification, not with reading the table. The census's instrument is reusable and cheap: mint exact-value probes from rendered pages (fan the routine reading out to fresh-context agents; ~5 probes/page), score by substring against each extractor, then spend the lead's own eyes ONLY on all-miss and disagreement rows — and before any eyeballing, run typography-normalised matching (curly→straight quotes, unicode spaces), because ~a quarter of apparent all-misses were byte-form drift, itself a real quote-out finding: extractors change typography even when extraction is perfect, so verbatim ⌘F contracts need normalised matching. Two operational mechanisms from the same day: the harness killed a ~53-minute background grind with no OOM and no error trail, so any long sweep wants resumability designed in (skip-completed-rows on rerun) rather than bolted on after the kill; and `git commit` without path arguments commits the whole index — which bites precisely in the stage-everything-before-red-controls workflow, where staging is a safety step and the sweep arrives later wearing an unrelated commit message.
